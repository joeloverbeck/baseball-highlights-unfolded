# Competitive rules

Page numbers refer to the Bases Loaded v5 rulebook (RB) and reference booklet (Ref). Resolve edition disputes using [the sources register](sources.md). A mini-game represents highlights. A card play can stop a previous threat and generate several new ones. A baseball simulation that counts pitches, outs or real innings would give different results.

## Series and setup · RB 2–5, 12

Choose distinct team decks, each with ten rookies and five veterans. Shuffle each 15-card Lineup and the 60 original Free Agents separately. Reveal six agents. With Free Agent expansions the designer recommends ten available cards. Record the selected packs and row size for the series. Start both runs and games won at zero. Home/Visitor governs first play and several later decisions.

| Format | Before the series | Series |
|---|---|---|
| Standard (eventual default) | Three buys. Each manager draws six and uses their revenue, alternates Home for the buys, and Visitor breaks a revenue tie by choosing purchase order. Purchased cards go atop the Lineup, demotions to Minors, remaining revenue cards to Dugout. | Random first Home, best of seven using the 2–3–2 home schedule. |
| Beginner | Play all three preliminary games, random first Home then alternate, with a buy after each even if the preliminary winner is decided. | Preliminary winner hosts games 1–2 and 6–7. Other team hosts 3–5. First to four wins. |
| Expert | Random first Home, begin with the starting rosters. | Alternate Home each game, first to six wins in up to eleven. Buy between games. Coaches first usable in game three. |

Runner locations and runs belong to this mini-game. Wins, active decks, deck ordering, purchases, demotions and applicable series effects carry forward. Preserve a final result before resetting a score display.

## Hand, timing and running · RB 3, 5–7, 15 and Ref 1–3, 12

Draw six from your Lineup. You may set one aside face down On Deck, then draw its replacement. The owner can inspect it. Both Dugouts are inspectable. Shuffle the Dugout into the Lineup only upon a required draw from an empty Lineup. Preserve deck order between games.

Visitor plays first. Alternate one play to each team’s In Play stack until six per side. For each play, (1) resolve its applicable Immediate Action, (2) settle surviving threats from the opponent’s last play in printed left-to-right order, then (3) place one batter per hit box on the active side in order, retaining speed and hit value. Added threats follow the printed boxes unless the effect specifies otherwise. Immediate hits score or move runners at once. Ordinary cancellations affect threats. Runners persist across turns.

For each hit, move existing runners in descending base order. White/slow advances the hit distance. Blue/average does likewise, except a single sends it home from second. Red/fast advances one extra base. Stop behind an occupied base, preserve order, score and remove each runner reaching home. The batter takes exactly the hit base regardless of speed. A home run brings everyone home. Process successive hits independently so a batter placed by one can gain its speed bonus on the next. Walks advance only a chain forced by the incoming batter.

| Base action | Required behavior |
|---|---|
| Glove | Choose a threatened hit to cancel. |
| Fastball, Curve, Spit Ball | Cancel all opposing hit boxes against a Natural, Robot or Cyborg respectively. |
| Knuckleball | Reduce each box one rank. A single disappears. |
| Double Play, Pick Off | Remove up to two slow/average runners for Double Play. Pick Off selects up to the printed count of opposing runners. Both target occupied bases. |
| Clutch | With a runner on second or third, advance all runners by the clutch hit distance plus one regardless of speed, then place a new runner of the played card’s speed on the hit base. |
| Leadoff, Quick Eye | Execute the printed immediate hits on your own first play, or against an opposing topmost Cyborg respectively. Normal hit boxes still follow. |
| Stolen Base | Advance average and fast runners one, highest base first, respecting blockers. |
| Walk | Convert each opposing hit box to a walk. Resolve them one at a time. |

Pinch hitting replaces the active play. Choose a PH-marked card from hand for the Dugout and use either your known On Deck card or the unseen top of your Lineup. If a shuffle is required, shuffle before discarding that PH card. The incoming card resolves normally and cannot chain another ordinary PH substitution.

After Home’s sixth play, Visitor can decline the Save, reveal On Deck, or reveal the unseen Lineup top, even just to cycle a card. Resolve only eligible defensive actions, discard the Save card to the Dugout, then settle remaining Home threats. Its hit boxes and revenue never enter play.

## Ties, cleanup and buying · RB 7–9

Keep existing runners on a tie. Draw three each. Both secretly commit one card, with the normal PH option, then reveal simultaneously and place both sets of threats. Resolve Home’s immediate effects first, Visitor’s second, then both sets of surviving hits. Compare only after both teams resolve. Repeat with remaining cards, then another group of three if needed. If the whole deck is exhausted and still tied, recycle played extra-inning cards per RB 8. Cap revenue at the entire roster’s combined revenue.

Credit the winner once. Each manager chooses whether unused On Deck goes atop the Lineup or into the Dugout. Clear runners and reset runs for the next game. Discard unused extra-inning hand cards. Played extra-inning cards contribute revenue in two-team play.

Total each side’s In Play revenue. Lower total chooses who shops first. On a tie the losing manager chooses. The buyer can pass or buy repeatedly within budget. Replace each bought card in the public row immediately before asking for the next purchase. Each addition goes face down atop that manager’s Lineup and requires a demotion from their In Play cards to the face-down Minor Leagues. Maintain 15 active players and preserve ordering when several join. Put remaining In Play cards in the Dugout and expire unspent funds. Show the arithmetic, the replacement and the new roster. The next hand draws through the actual modified Lineup.

## Verification examples

- RB 10–11 opens with Turtlebot threatening two singles. Mickey Maris gloves one. One blue runner reaches first. A PH discard brings in Satchel Seaver, whose Fastball cancels both of Mickey’s Natural threats. Check each intermediate zone and threat.
- RB 15 puts a slow runner on second and a fast one on first. On a single, they end on third and second. With an average runner on second and a clear path, that runner instead scores.
- Exercise an empty-Lineup PH, a declined and an ineffective Visitor Save, and a tied extra inning with both commitments hidden until reveal. Inspect public and private observations at each decision.
- In a buy with two affordable signings, the first refill must be eligible as the second purchase. Verify two demotions, total spent, roster size, next hand and a resume between purchases. Keep card-specific source references and expected outcomes with implementation evidence.
